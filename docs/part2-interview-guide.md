# Part 2: user interview guide (5–6 interviews)

Purpose: test what the discovery engine found in public feedback, and fill the gaps it can't cover (how people discover products, who experiments, what would make them try a new category). Each finding gets a verdict (confirmed / contradicted / unclear) that goes on the Validation page as check **V9**.

Time per interview: 25–30 minutes. Remote (video or phone) is fine. Record only with consent; never put names in the deck.

---

## 1. Hypotheses to test (from the engine, 24 Nov 2026)

| # | Hypothesis | Engine evidence | What would contradict it |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | **Fear of a bad product with no easy return** is the main reason people don't try a new category | Return/refund friction in 29% of tagged entries; largest theme "No clear return or refund process" (122) | Interviewees try new categories freely and don't mention returns |
| H2 | **Freshness and expiry doubts** stop people buying fresh and perishable categories | Expiry/freshness in 26%; two food-freshness themes (45 + 35) | Perishables are bought without hesitation; issues are about other things |
| H3 | **Quality and authenticity doubts** grow with price and unfamiliar brands (beauty, electronics, baby) | Quality/trust doubts in 49%; fake-product fears in reviews | Trust is the same across categories |
| H4 | **Habit / speed of reordering** keeps baskets narrow: people open the app for a known list | Habit stated in 8% of entries; "buy again" is the only discovery channel mentioned | People browse regularly and buy outside their list |
| H5 | **Price and fees** matter less than trust for trying something new | Price/fees in 12% vs trust 49% | Price is the first thing people bring up |
| H6 | People don't know many categories are sold on Blinkit | "Unaware it is sold" barely appears in reviews (reviews can't show this) | Everyone knows the full range |

Questions the data could **not** answer (ask openly): how people discover new products today; which kinds of users experiment; what information they want before trying.

## 2. Who to talk to (screener)

Aim for a mix across 5–6 people:

| Criterion | Target |
|---|---|
| Uses Blinkit (or Zepto/Instamart) | At least twice a month in the last 3 months |
| Category behaviour | 3 who mostly reorder the same things, 2–3 who have tried something new recently |
| Life stage | Mix: student, working professional, parent; ideally one pet owner |
| City | At least one outside the biggest metros if possible |
| Exclusions | People working at quick-commerce companies |

Screener questions (2 minutes):
1. How often do you order from Blinkit or similar apps?
2. Roughly what do you usually order? (note the categories)
3. In the last month, did you buy anything from a category you hadn't bought from before on the app?
4. Who do you live with? (helps the segment mix)

---

## 3. Script

**Intro (2 min).** "I'm studying how people shop on quick-commerce apps. There are no right answers. I'll ask about your own experiences. Is it okay if I take notes / record? Nothing will be shared with your name."

**A. Current habits (5 min)**
- Walk me through your last order. What did you buy? Why then? How did you find each item?
- When you open the app, do you usually know what you want? How often do you browse?
- What do you *never* buy on the app, and why? *(listen for H2, H3, H6)*

**B. A new-category story (7 min)**
- Tell me about the last time you bought something on the app you'd never bought there before. What made you try it? How did you find it? Was it worth it?
- If nothing comes to mind: think of something you buy elsewhere (a pharmacy, a pet shop, a beauty store). Why not on the app? *(H1–H3, H6)*

**C. Barriers (6 min)**, only after they've answered freely; read neutrally:
- If a product from a new category arrived and you didn't like it, what would you expect to happen? Have you tried returning something? How did it go? *(H1)*
- How do you decide if fresh items will be good? Has expiry or freshness ever stopped you? *(H2)*
- Are there things you'd only buy from a brand or store you trust? Why? *(H3)*
- Does price or the delivery fee change what you're willing to try? *(H5)*

**D. Discovery and information (5 min)**
- How do you usually find out a product exists on the app: search, the home screen, suggestions, friends, social media?
- Before trying something new, what would you want to see: reviews, ingredients, expiry date, return policy, photos, price comparison?

**E. Wrap-up (3 min)**
- If you could change one thing so you'd try more kinds of products on the app, what would it be?
- Show (or describe) one idea: *"First order in any new category: easy one-tap return or refund, no questions."* Would that change anything for you? Why / why not? *(tests the H1 direction without leading earlier)*

Probing lines throughout: "Tell me more." "Why?" "Can you give an example?" "What happened next?"
